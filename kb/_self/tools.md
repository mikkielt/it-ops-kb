# Tools

Every tool is stdlib-only Python in `_tools/`, run as `python3 _tools/<tool>`. Each tool's docstring (`--help`) is its full reference; this file is the one table of what exists and how the lookup tools decide. The read tools span every root (`kb/public` and any root a team adds; `_tools/kbcommon.py`, "Roots") and name files and topics by qualified path, `<root>/<path in root>` (`public/intune/win32-apps.md:12`); `--root NAME` (MCP: `root`) keeps one root. Lookup rules for agents are in `AGENTS.md`. Skills and subagents read the part of a doc they need with `python3 _tools/selfdoc.py section DOC HEADING` (the `selfdoc.py` row below), not the whole file; skills read Conduct that way, and the other rules are asked for with `python3 _tools/rag.py pack --root _self` (the `rag.py pack` row below). Before reading a tool's source, run `python3 _tools/selfdoc.py map-tools FILE` (or grep the full map for a name): it gives each function, class, method and test with `path:line` and its first docstring line, so only the lines of the symbol found need reading (`rag.py show PATH:LINE -n N` or `sed -n`).

## How the lookup tools decide

- **One parser.** `pack`, `facts`, `audit` and `src --cited` read the kb through `_tools/kbfacts.py`, which parses fact units (bullets, table rows, data rows), tags (grammar in its docstring) and ledger entries one way for every tool.
- **The verdict.** `pack` ranks fact lines (BM25 with light stemming) and prints `coverage: none` when the question's named terms are not in the kb or most key words are missing, `weak` when the best article matches under 60% of the key words, else `good`. A word counts as a key word only when under a fifth of the lines of all roots together hold it, or when a single line holds it, so a small root served alone keeps its key words [CODE _tools/kbfacts.py#informative_words]. A line holds the words of its file name, article title, section and text; its directory name ranks it but is not held, so a growing domain does not turn its own name (`agents`) into a common word. It also holds the parts of its compounds (hyphen and dot parts, identifier parts), and a number joined to a unit by a hyphen as the number itself, so a question's `1.5 seconds` matches a fact's `1.5-second`, and a version a model id spells with a hyphen as the dotted one, so `Opus 5.5` matches the `claude-opus-5-5` row of `claude/models.csv` [CODE _tools/kbfacts.py#terms]. The article whose one printable line holds the most of those key words leads the pack, and the other articles keep their order by score, so a data row that holds a question's model, columns and price words comes before prose facts that each hold a rare word or two; within a data file the rows print by score, not in file order, so the budget cuts the weakest row first, where an article's facts print in article order [CODE _tools/kbfacts.py#pack]. Untagged lines (printed `(no tag)`) can lift `none` to `weak`, never to `good`. `rag.py eval` checks this against `kb/public/_retrieval/lookup_eval.csv`; `tests.py` runs it. [CODE _tools/kbfacts.py#pack]
- **The rules route (`--root _self`).** `pack` and `search` read the top-level `kb/_self/*.md` docs as sentence passages instead of fact lines. A line longer than a passage is split at its sentences, and a sentence that is still too long at its list separators. A passage after the first of a line carries the line's lead (the bold lead of a bullet, else its first clause) in front of its text, and a nested list item carries the end of its parent's last sentence. A table row's passages each start with its first cell. The first cell of a table row and the bold lead of a bullet weigh like a title. The verdict is `good` only when the question matches a tested question of the eval file (at least three quarters of the question's key words are in it and at least half of its own key words in the question, a word of fewer than three letters never counting), and the passage that tested question is anchored to is printed first, marked `tested`; otherwise it is `weak` or `none`. A pack with a tested match prints its pinned passages and at most three further passages, whatever the budget leaves, so a tested answer is short; a `weak` pack keeps the whole budget, since the reader must find the line itself [CODE _tools/kbfacts.py#pick_passages]. A passage longer than the print width is cut at it, unless the words that answer lie beyond the cut: it then prints as the window around the tested question's anchor phrase (else around the densest cluster of the question's key words), with `...` at each cut end [CODE _tools/kbfacts.py#clip_around]. A tested question is an expansion of its anchored passage: it weighs like a doc2query expansion and never adds a key word. The informative words of each anchored tested question are computed once per store view [CODE _tools/kbfacts.py#tested_matches]. The active decisions of `kb/_self/_decisions.csv` come after the passages, each tied to the docs or sections whose passages are printed by an `article:_self/<doc>[#<Heading>]` context reference; for a question, such a tied decision prints only when its text or its source holds a key word of the question or of the tested question pinned, so a section's decisions do not follow every question that touches the section [CODE _tools/kbfacts.py#pack]. `--item ID` builds the question from the item's title and goal, takes as candidate docs those `kb/_self/map.csv` names for the item's `touches` (every rule doc when none), and adds the decisions whose context is `item:ID`. `--set NAME` prints, with no ranking and no verdict, the anchored passage of each row whose `sets` cell names `NAME` (`skill`, or `skill:step`, a bare skill name taking all its steps), then the decisions tied to those passages. `rag.py eval --root _self` checks the route against `kb/_self/_retrieval/lookup_eval.csv`; `tests.py` runs it. [CODE _tools/kbfacts.py#self_units]
- **Two corrections to the verdict.** Both are lexical and cheap, and neither demotes a true `good` of the eval set ("Retrieval quality" in `kb/_self/reports/benchmarks.md`). [CODE _tools/kbfacts.py#specific]
  - *Common words only:* a `good` becomes `weak` when no single tagged fact holds every key word and the question names nothing specific: no name (a capital or a digit), identifier (`list/get`, `python_files`, `--frozen`, `gitlab-runner`), one-word product alias (`dsc`), word an article is about (in its title or file name, with 60% of the word's lines: `ruff`, `krbtgt`) or word the kb itself mostly spells as a brand (`cmpivot` -> CMPivot). "email attachment size" and "mailbox size limit" are `weak`; "battery health report" stays `good` (one fact holds all three).
  - *Another product:* the verdict becomes `none` when a name the question uses is held by under 1% of all lines and by none of the lines the pack could print (VMware Horizon, SAP GUI, Zscaler). An unknown name of 4 or more letters among well-matched words (NinjaOne) is also `none`; the exemption stays for abbreviations (AV, PC).
- **The `check:` line (a possible false `good`).** The verdict counts words, not meaning. Under a `good` verdict the pack prints `check:` when a name the question uses (a product, an id) appears nowhere in the lead article, or when no single fact holds half of 4+ key words. The `kb:` hook then passes the pack to the model instead of answering alone. What still passes both corrections, all on the off-kb list (`kb/public/_retrieval/doc2query/offkb_questions.txt`): common words that one unrelated fact holds together ("password reset link" in the KRBTGT article, "page file size" in a remediations fact) and a capitalised generic name the lead article happens to hold ("Remote Desktop Gateway" in Power BI Desktop and the data gateway), both without a `check:` line; another product the kb mentions on a printable line ("ServiceNow with Intune"), with one. A lexical rule would need to know which word is the subject; the rules tried and what they cost are in "Verdict corrections" in `kb/_self/reports/benchmarks.md`.
- **The `freshness:` line.** A question about the latest release (latest, newest, current version) or naming a version the kb never mentions (`2.2.365`) gets `freshness: these facts are as of <lead article's retrieved_utc>`, and "check the cited source live and say which version your answer is for". Without it, models reported the kb's newest release as the latest ("Partial knowledge, newer versions and stale copies" in `kb/_self/reports/benchmarks.md`). [CODE _tools/kbfacts.py#freshness]
- **The route lines.** A `weak` or `none` pack, and a `good` pack with a `check:` line or a word held nowhere in the kb, prints `route: split|web`, `kb has:` and `kb lacks:` after the coverage, `check:`, `freshness:` and none-sentence lines and before the first article (the none sentence says the kb does not cover the question and tells the agent to state what the kb has and lacks and to research the rest in the live docs, never from memory [CODE _tools/kbfacts.py#NONE_SENTENCE]); a clean `good` pack prints none of them, so its text is unchanged. `none` routes `web`, the rest `split`, except a near miss: a `none` whose words held nowhere in the kb are all language or format names (T-SQL, PowerShell, Python, Bash, KQL, JSON, YAML, XML, CSV) routes `split`, since the kb may hold the subject and lack only the syntax (the `sp_getapplock` question, `not in the kb: t-sql`), and prints no none sentence but the fact lines and source footer a `weak` pack would, so the reader can answer the part the kb has; a best article's share of key words does not decide the route [CODE _tools/kbfacts.py#LANGUAGE_NAMES]. `kb has:` lists the question's own words, in its order, that the best article matches, `kb lacks:` the informative ones it does not (`-` when empty). A several-part pack starts with one overall route line: `web` when every part routes web, else `split` when any part routes [CODE _tools/kbfacts.py#route_of].- **Decisions beside the facts.** Decisions are rows of `_decisions.csv` (`kb/_self/content-rules.md`, "Decisions"), not facts, so `kbfacts.decision_rows()` reads them apart from the index (a decision edit rebuilds nothing) and `pack`, `show` and `audit` print them [CODE _tools/kbfacts.py#decisions_for] [CODE _tools/kbfacts.py#show_decisions] [CODE _tools/kbfacts.py#decision_conflicts].
  - *Which are printed.* A pack prints at most 3 decision lines, in a `## decisions` section after the articles: an active one as `decided by <by> on <date>`, a proposed one as `proposed (not confirmed)`, and with `--invalidated` (MCP `include_invalidated: true`) an invalidated one as `invalidated because <reason>`; a superseded one never. A decision is printed when it *answers* the question, or is *tied* to what the pack prints and holds one of the question's informative words. Tied is: a printed fact cites it (`[DECISION id]`) or it names the fact (`fact:`; both print with no word needed), or its `article:` is a printed article, its `domain:` holds one, or its `source:` is cited by a printed fact. An invalidated decision tied to a printed article needs no word. A root's decisions are tied to its own articles, those of `kb/_self` to the articles they name (`article:public/auth/kerberos`), and a server limited to named roots reads no `kb/_self` decision. Each line is `PATH:LINE label: text [DECISION id]`, the row `rag.py show PATH:LINE` prints.
  - *Coverage.* A decision answers a question when its text holds 60% of the question's informative words (the words that tell units apart, as for facts) and every name the question uses (a capital or a digit), counted lexically with the same stemming as facts [CODE _tools/kbfacts.py#DECISION_ANSWER_SHARE]. An active one that answers makes the pack `good` whatever the facts say (its words no longer count as missing, no `check:` line follows from the facts, no route line when nothing else is missing, and the line reads `coverage: good (an active decision answers it ...)`); a proposed or an invalidated one never changes the verdict, so a question only a proposal answers stays `weak` or `none` and prints the proposal for what it is. No decision rows: the pack is byte for byte what it was.
  - *Budget.* The lines are charged to the pack's budget before the facts and cost at most a third of it, so they never add to the size [CODE _tools/kbfacts.py#DECISION_BUDGET_SHARE].
  - *`show`.* After the lines of an article or data file `show` prints `decisions:` and the decisions cited by a fact that starts in the shown range or naming one, and those whose `article:` is the file or whose `domain:` holds it.
  - *`audit`.* After the table, `possible contradiction: N active decisions share <context>` and the rows, once per shared context reference (an article's or a domain's value qualified, `article:public/auth/kerberos`); proposed, invalidated and superseded decisions are not listed. A prefix or `--root` narrows them as it does the articles. It is for a person to read: no check fails and nothing is blocked. `--json` audit prints the article rows only.
- **Observed signals beside the facts.** The active rows of `_logs.csv` (`kb/_self/content-rules.md`, "Logs") are context for a lookup, not facts: `kbfacts.parse_tag` knows the `LOG` kind (`[LOG <log row id>]`, the id in the part's `log`, never in its source `ids`), but `tags_in` leaves it out, so a unit's `tags`, audit's fact and tag counts and a pack's `good` never see it, and a fact whose only tag is a `LOG` one is a fact with no tag [CODE _tools/kbfacts.py#tags_in] [CODE _tools/kbfacts.py#log_ids_in]. `kbfacts.log_rows()` reads the files apart from the index like the decisions (a server limited to named roots reads no `kb/_self` file; a public root holds none, so such a lookup prints nothing extra) [CODE _tools/kbfacts.py#log_rows].
  - *Which are printed.* Only an `active` row (a proposed or an invalidated one never) whose context is tied to what the lookup prints, tied as for a decision: a printed fact cites it (`[LOG id]`) or its `fact:` is a printed fact, its `article:` is a printed article, its `domain:` holds one, or its `source:` is cited by a printed fact; a row about an `item:` alone is never tied [CODE _tools/kbfacts.py#logs_for]. A pack and `show` print at most 2 lines, `audit` a larger cap, the latest observed day first, under `observed signal (LOG, not a fact):` after the facts and decisions, each `PATH:LINE observed <from> to <to>: <observation> [LOG id]` [CODE _tools/kbfacts.py#log_line].
  - *Never coverage.* The lines are computed after the verdict, the route and the `check:` lines, which are those of the same lookup with no `_logs.csv`; a LOG line is evidence of what was observed about the lookups, never the answer, so a `none` that routes `web` prints none. They take what budget the facts leave, so a small budget drops them first and the fact lines never change [CODE _tools/kbfacts.py#pack]. The planted failures are the tests named `lookup_shows_log_lines` and `log_not_counted_in_coverage`.
- **The index.** `pack`, `search`, the `kb:` hook and the MCP server read one persisted index: postings lists in a stdlib `sqlite3` file named after a fingerprint of the kb files' content. It lives in `_cache/` in a clone, the main worktree's for every linked worktree of the clone (`${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_DATA}` for an installed plugin, a temp directory if neither is writable; `KB_INDEX=DIR` elsewhere, `KB_INDEX=0` in memory only), is never committed, and is rebuilt the first time after any kb file changes; a cold `pack` then takes about 0.1 s. A rebuild weighs again only the units whose inputs changed: `weighed()` keeps each unit's words and weights in a unit cache beside the index, one file per root set named as the index files are (`kbunits.sqlite` for this repository's roots alone, else `kbunits-r<hash>.sqlite`), so a build over fewer roots never prunes the rows of another set's, with a row keyed by `INDEX_VERSION`, the doc2query mode, the weighing code (the title, Summary and expansion weights, both `STOP` sets, the word patterns, and a digest of the compiled code of `_weigh`, `terms`, `stem` and `bare`, which ignores comments and line numbers) and everything it reads for the unit (path, title or lead, section, text, its article's Summary, its expansions, its tested questions), so an edit to the weighing re-weighs every unit with no `INDEX_VERSION` bump, and drops the rows no unit used once they pass twice the units weighed, then runs `VACUUM` so the file gives the freed pages back (a `DELETE` alone leaves its size; the prune is rare, since the rows must double first) (the docs state no size or count of the cache or the index: both grow with the kb, `ls -l _cache` shows them); the postings are written in term order. On the Windows host a first build takes about 11 s and a `pack` after an edit to one article, or to `_tools/kbfacts.py` outside the weighing code, about 4.7 s (`kb/_self/reports/test-suite-speed.md`) [CODE _tools/kbfacts.py#weighed]. The fingerprint is git's blob ids of the files (a modified or untracked file, and one marked assume-unchanged or skip-worktree, which git never reports modified, hashed as git would), so it depends on neither the checkout's path nor the files' times: a fresh worktree of a clone finds the index its clone built for the same content (first `pack` under 1 s instead of 14 s on that host), a file touched without a change keeps it, and an edit of any indexed file gives a new one [CODE _tools/kbfacts.py#content_key]. Those two git calls run only when the files' times and sizes, taken from the directory listings rather than one `stat` per file (about 0.7 ms a file in a Hyper-V container), changed since the checkout last asked: a memo file per checkout (`kbkey-<hash>.txt` beside the index) maps them to the key, which is kept 2 s after it was computed [CODE _tools/kbfacts.py#fingerprint]. A new index removes only the root set's index files beyond the 4 most recently used that were unused for 15 minutes; a process marks its file used when it opens it and once a minute while it serves from it, so one worktree's build never deletes another's current index. The index files and the unit cache of any other root set go once unused for 14 days (`PRUNE_OTHER_ROOTS`; opening a unit cache marks it used too), however many there are, so `_cache` holds at most the 4 plus the current file of this root set and one file set per root set in use [CODE _tools/kbfacts.py#prune_indexes].
- **Where a pack's time goes.** A warm pack is the ranking (`rank`) and the lines it prints; a cold process adds the index open. What a pack must not do is repeat, per call, work that depends on the view alone. The `_self` view is held on the base store (`Store.view`), as the main and index views are, so its postings lists are read once per process, and `tested_matches` computes the informative words of each anchored tested question once per view (`Store._anchor_words`), not once per pack; before that, `rag.py eval` over the `_self` rows made a postings read per word of every anchor on every pack, most of the run's `_load_own` calls and a third of its time. A view derived from the base (`Subset`, `Prefix`) filters the base's list in `own` and reads no index file. The eval run's times sit in `kb/_self/reports/benchmarks.md` (Tool speed), and `python3 -m cProfile -s cumtime _tools/rag.py eval` shows the next cost [CODE _tools/kbfacts.py#tested_matches] [CODE _tools/kbfacts.py#Store].
- **What search sees.** `search` ranks every line (facts, prose paragraphs, code blocks, data rows) the way `pack` does, at most 2 hits per file. It skips the index files (`README.md`, each root's `_answers.md`, `_gaps.md`, `_conflicts.md`, `_coverage.csv`) and the `kb/_self/` docs; `--index` includes them [CODE _tools/kbfacts.py#index_files]. The pack never sees them. `--json` gives machine output: each hit carries `path`, `line`, `heading`, `text` and the source ids it cites, and with `-u` also `urls`.
- **The `kb:` hook.** `kb: <question>` is answered from the pack without calling the model when coverage is `good` with no `check:` line and no `route:` line; otherwise the prompt goes to the model with context. A pack with a `route:` line (the route lines above) gets the differential: one instruction to answer what the kb has from the pack with `path:line` and urls, to research only what it lacks in the live docs, to label that part "live docs, not in the kb" with its url and never to fill it from memory, then the pack for `split` or, for `web`, only its coverage, `check:`, `freshness:`, `route:`, `kb has:` and `kb lacks:` lines with no fact lines. The pack is cut at a line boundary so the context stays under the 10,000 characters Claude Code keeps (`kb/public/claude/hooks.md`); the hook stays free of the model and the network. `kb+: <question>` always goes to the model: a clean `good` pack with a short instruction, a routed pack as for `kb:` [CODE _tools/kb_hook.py#routed]. `backlog:` is answered by the same hook without the model, with the output of `backlog.py horizon` and `backlog.py next --all --any` as the reason; `backlog+:` passes that output to the model as context, cut to the same limit: each command keeps an equal share of it (a short output leaves the rest to the other), cut at line boundaries and ending in a note of how many lines were left out and the command that prints them all, so a long `horizon` never drops the `next` list [CODE _tools/kb_hook.py#fit_blocks]. A failing or timed-out command never blocks the prompt: the model gets a note naming it. Neither prefix writes a query log row or loads the facts. `AGENTS.md` routes a plain-words question about open work and its order to those two commands, or to the `backlog:` prefix, so no search of the codebase is needed.
- **`kb_ask.py` routing.** Counts and "who cites" questions go to `audit`/`src` (no model). Numbered parts or several questions are split, one pack each (up to 6). `good` goes to Haiku with the packs and no tools (`claude -p` without user plugins and MCP servers), which replies `INSUFFICIENT: ...` when the facts are only related, escalating. A pack with a `route:` line plans by it (`--route` prints `kind=web` or `kind=split` with the route, `kb has:` and `kb lacks:` lines): `web` goes to one Sonnet researcher at low effort with the kb server alone (its cached `docs_search` and `docs_fetch`, so a repeated live-docs search within 7 days calls no docs server) and WebSearch/WebFetch, given the question, the `kb lacks:` line and up to three nearest article titles as leads to verify; its JSON result is printed, and an error result's errors go to stderr with exit 1. `split` runs two: the Haiku reader (no tools, JSON result) answers the `kb has:` part from the pack, and the researcher answers only the `kb lacks:` part without the pack; one answer is printed, the kb part and then the live part under "Live docs, not in the kb", and `-v` prints each run's cost and their sum. A reader's `INSUFFICIENT` sends the whole question to the researcher and sets `escalated`; the row's route stays `split`. A split pack without its `kb has:` or `kb lacks:` line is read whole and escalates as `good` does. In a several-part split each part goes where its own route sends it: a part with no `route:` line to the reader whole, a `web` part to the researcher whole (its `kb has:` words never reach the reader), a `split` part's `kb has:` words to the reader and its `kb lacks:` words to the researcher; `--route` lists each part's entries [CODE _tools/kb_ask.py#part_routes].
- **fact diff.** `factdiff.py detect` trusts a provider's signal only as far as `_tools/providers.csv` says it works (a 304 is always trusted; a 200 falls through to the version id, then the text hash). `apply` dates a source only when the text it was compared with is no newer than the source's last confirmation, by the detection baseline or the page's own content date (`updated_at` on Learn), and a fact found word for word only when its anchor was placed no later than that; the rest waits for `review` or the census's own checks. A source held back because its baseline postdates its last confirmation starts its dating chain at its next confirmation (a `/kb-refresh` or the regular census); no separate run is needed. The cut-offs live in `_tools/factdiff.toml`, each with its measurement in `kb/_self/reports/fact-diff.md`.
- **fetch.** `--diff` exits 0 when nothing changed, 1 when a source changed and 2 when a fetch or the selection failed, as `diff` does. `--max-lines N` caps each diff, `--no-save` compares without moving the baseline, `--delay` sets the per-host pause (default 1.1 s). HTML is reduced to its main text first, so page chrome does not count as a change. Only pinned urls carry `artifact_sha256` (raw files at a commit or tag, release downloads); for live pages the hash taken at retrieval sits in `version_or_date`.
