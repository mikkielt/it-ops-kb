# GDPR pseudonymisation: Art. 4(5), Recital 26, EDPB 01/2025, C-413/23 P

## Summary
- Art. 4(5) defines pseudonymisation. Recital 26 says pseudonymised data that can be attributed with additional information is data about an identifiable person, and that anonymous data is out of scope.
- EDPB Guidelines 01/2025 were adopted 2025-01-16 **as a version for public consultation**. No final version was published as of 2026-09-27. On 2026-07-08 the EDPB adopted separate guidelines on anonymisation (public consultation until 2026-10-30) that take C-413/23 P into account.
- CJEU C-413/23 P (EDPS v SRB), judgment of 2025-09-04: pseudonymised data is not always personal data, i.e. not in every case and for every person. Identifiability for the controller's duty to inform is assessed when the data is collected, from the controller's point of view.

## Facts
- Art. 4(5), as the EDPB Guidelines 01/2025 (paragraph 16) quote it: "the processing of personal data in such a manner that the personal data can no longer be attributed to a specific data subject without the use of additional information, provided that such additional information is kept separately and is subject to technical and organisational measures to ensure that the personal data are not attributed to an identified or identifiable natural person"; the EDPB's news item of 2025-01-17 quotes the same definition in a footnote. [DOC S-rebms33k, S873]
- Recital 26 (Rec. 26 GDPR in the EDPB Guidelines 01/2025): pseudonymised data that could be attributed to a natural person using additional information is to be considered information on an identifiable natural person, and so is personal data; identifiability takes into account the means reasonably likely to be used by the controller or by another person. The EDPB's news item of 2025-01-17 adds that if the data can be linked back to an individual by the data controller or someone else, it remains personal data. [DOC S-rebms33k, S873]
- Recital 28 (Rec. 28 GDPR in the EDPB Guidelines 01/2025): pseudonymising data reduces risks for data subjects, and the explicit introduction of pseudonymisation is not intended to preclude any other measures of data protection. The GDPR imposes no general obligation to use pseudonymisation. [DOC S-rebms33k]

Whether data is anonymous may vary from one entity to another; the framework tests three criteria (no record isolation, no linkage, no inference), applied either contextually (differences in capability between entities) or in a simplified way that ignores those differences. [DOC S-eickk3hp]
- C-413/23 P (EDPS v SRB), judgment 2025-09-04: the CJEU set aside General Court judgment T-557/20 and referred the case back, and held that pseudonymised data is not always personal data: not "in all cases and for every person". [DOC S875]
- In our own words: personal opinions in comments "relate" to their authors, with no need to examine content or effects. The General Court was right that pseudonymised data is not personal data in all cases and for every person, because pseudonymisation may stop people other than the controller from identifying the person. For the controller's duty to inform, identifiability is judged when the data is collected, from the controller's point of view, before any transfer. [DOC S875]
- The case applies Regulation (EU) 2018/1725 (EU institutions), not the GDPR directly. [DOC S875]
